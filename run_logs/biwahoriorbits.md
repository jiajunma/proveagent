## Problem Statement

*** Problem Statement ***


Classification of bi-Iwahori orbits on \(M_{n \times m}(F)\)

Setup

Let \(F\) be a non-archimedean local field with ring of integers \(\mathcal{O}\) and uniformizer \(\varphi\). Let \(n, m \geq 1\) be integers. The *Iwahori subgroup* \(I \subset \mathrm{GL}_n(F)\) is defined as the set of invertible \(n \times n\) matrices \(g = (g_{ij})\) such that:
\[
\mathrm{val}(g_{ij}) \geq 0 \;\; \text{for } i \leq j \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{val}(g_{ij}) \geq 1 \;\; \text{for } i > j.
\]
Similarly, the Iwahori subgroup \(I' \subset \mathrm{GL}_m(F)\) consists of invertible \(m \times m\) matrices with the same valuation conditions.

The groups \(\mathrm{GL}_n(F)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_m(F)\) act on the space of \(n \times m\) matrices \(M_{n \times m}(F)\) by
\[
(g,h) \cdot M = g M h^{-1}
\quad \text{for } g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(F),\; h \in \mathrm{GL}_m(F),\; M \in M_{n \times m}(F).
\]
Restricting to the Iwahori subgroups, we obtain the *bi-Iwahori action* of \(I \times I'\) on \(M_{n \times m}(F)\).

Let \(E_{ij}\) denote the elementary matrix with \(1\) at position \((i,j)\) and \(0\) elsewhere.

A *\(\mathbb{Z}\)-colored partial matching* is a subset \(\sigma \subseteq [n] \times [m] \times \mathbb{Z}\) such that each row index \(i \in [n]\) appears in at most one triple of \(\sigma\), and each column index \(j \in [m]\) appears in at most one triple of \(\sigma\). Equivalently, the pairs \((i,j)\) occurring in \(\sigma\) form a partial matching between \([n]\) and \([m]\).

For such \(\sigma\), define
\[
E_\sigma := \sum_{(i,j,v) \in \sigma} \varphi^v E_{ij}.
\]
(Here \([n] := \{1, \ldots, n\}\) and \([m] := \{1, \ldots, m\}\).)

**Goal:** Prove that the bi-Iwahori orbits on \(M_{n \times m}(F)\) are classified by \(\mathbb{Z}\)-colored partial matchings. Precisely:
1. *Surjectivity:* Every matrix in \(M_{n \times m}(F)\) lies in the orbit of \(E_\sigma\) for some \(\mathbb{Z}\)-colored partial matching \(\sigma\).
2. *Distinctness:* For \(\mathbb{Z}\)-colored partial matchings \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\), the matrices \(E_\sigma\) and \(E_\tau\) lie in the same orbit if and only if \(\sigma = \tau\).

Let $I \subset \mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ and $I' \subset \mathrm{GL}_m(F)$ be the Iwahori subgroups. #### 1. Surjectivity: The Reduction Algorithm

We prove surjectivity by providing a deterministic algorithm that reduces any matrix $M \in M_{n \times m}(F)$ to a form $E_\sigma$ using only operations from $I \times I'$.

**Total Order on Positions:**
Define a total order $\prec$ on the set of matrix positions $[n] \times [m]$:
$$(i,j) \prec (i',j') \iff (j < j') \text{ or } (j=j' \text{ and } i > i').$$
This order proceeds column by column from left to right, and within each column, from bottom to top.

**The Algorithm:**
The algorithm is recursive. Let $M \in M_{n \times m}(F)$.

1.  **Base Case:** If $M$ is the zero matrix, the algorithm terminates. The output is $E_\emptyset$.

2.  **Pivot Selection:** If $M$ is non-zero, let $v_1 = \min_{i,j} \mathrm{val}(M_{ij})$. Let $(i_1, j_1)$ be the unique position that is minimal with respect to $\prec$ among all positions $(i,j)$ where $\mathrm{val}(M_{ij}) = v_1$.

3.  **Normalization:** Let $M_{i_1, j_1} = u \varphi^{v_1}$ where $u \in \mathcal{O}^\times$. Let $D \in I$ be the diagonal matrix with $u^{-1}$ at position $(i_1, i_1)$ and $1$s elsewhere. Let $M_1 = D M$. Then $(M_1)_{i_1, j_1} = \varphi^{v_1}$, and the pivot position and minimum valuation remain unchanged.

4.  **Annihilation:** We use Iwahori operations to annihilate the non-pivot entries in row $i_1$ and column $j_1$ of $M_1$.
    *   **Column Annihilation (Left Multiplication):** For each $i \neq i_1$, we perform the row operation $R_i \to R_i - c_i R_{i_1}$ with $c_i = (M_1)_{i,j_1} / \varphi^{v_1}$. The matrix for this operation is $g_i = I - c_i E_{i,i_1}$.
        *   For $i > i_1$: The pivot choice implies $(i,j_1) \prec (i_1,j_1)$, so $\mathrm{val}((M_1)_{i,j_1}) > v_1$. Thus, $\mathrm{val}(c_i) \ge 1$. Since $i>i_1$, $g_i \in I$.
        *   For $i < i_1$: We have $\mathrm{val}((M_1)_{i,j_1}) \ge v_1$, so $\mathrm{val}(c_i) \ge 0$. Since $i<i_1$, $g_i \in I$.
    *   **Row Annihilation (Right Multiplication):** For each $j \neq j_1$, we perform the column operation $C_j \to C_j - d_j C_{j_1}$ with $d_j = (M_1)_{i_1,j} / \varphi^{v_1}$. This corresponds to right multiplication by $h_j^{-1} = I - d_j E_{j_1,j}$.
        *   For $j < j_1$: The pivot choice implies $(i_1,j) \prec (i_1,j_1)$, so $\mathrm{val}((M_1)_{i_1,j}) > v_1$. Thus, $\mathrm{val}(d_j) \ge 1$. Since $j_1 > j$, $h_j^{-1} \in I'$.
        *   For $j > j_1$: We have $\mathrm{val}((M_1)_{i_1,j}) \ge v_1$, so $\mathrm{val}(d_j) \ge 0$. Since $j_1 < j$, $h_j^{-1} \in I'$.

5.  **Recursion:** Let $M'_{\text{sub}}$ be the submatrix of $M'$ obtained by removing row $i_1$ and column $j_1$. Apply the algorithm to $M'_{\text{sub}}$. Any Iwahori operation on the submatrix (acting on rows/columns with indices in $[n]\setminus\{i_1\}$ and $[m]\setminus\{j_1\}$) corresponds to a valid Iwahori operation on the full matrix, which does not alter row $i_1$ or column $j_1$.

The algorithm terminates, producing a matrix $E_\sigma = \sum_{k=1}^r \varphi^{v_k} E_{i_k, j_k}$. By construction, for any starting matrix $M$, there exist $G \in I, H \in I'$ such that $E_\sigma = G M H^{-1}$. This proves surjectivity.

**Monotonicity of Valuations:** Let $v_k$ be the valuation of the $k$-th pivot. At step $k$, we have a matrix $M^{(k-1)}$ and select a pivot with valuation $v_k = \min \mathrm{val}(M^{(k-1)})$. The entries of $R^{(k)}$ are entries of the matrix obtained from $M^{(k-1)}$ by Iwahori operations. Since all operations involve matrices in $\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O})$, the minimum valuation cannot decrease. Thus, all entries of $R^{(k)}$ have valuation at least $v_k$.
